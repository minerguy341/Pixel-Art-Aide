# Quick study — stone before vs after cutting & polishing

Scope: how a stone's surface changes from raw/quarried → cut → honed → polished, and how to paint
that difference (directly feeds a raw-vs-dressed Arcane Stone pair).

## Findings
- **Polishing "consolidates" reflection — it doesn't add pigment.** A rough surface scatters light in
  MANY directions (diffuse → looks lighter/matte/chalky from every angle). A polished surface reflects in
  ONE direction → looks **darker from most angles** but throws a **bright specular hotspot** from a few.
  So polished = darker base + a bright sheen streak; raw = uniformly lighter/duller.
- **Polishing raises saturation & depth** — brings out full colour, "maximum definition of veining,
  pattern and grain," greatest chromatic depth. Raw stone is duller, chalkier, washed-out.
- **Polishing reveals internal structure** — grinding/buffing exposes the grain, mineral pattern and
  **veins**; raw/pitted surface hides them.
- **The finish spectrum:** rough/quarried (irregular, matte, pale, pitted, **broken rounded edges**) →
  **sawn/cut** (flat geometric faces, still matte, saw texture) → **honed** (smooth even matte, low sheen)
  → **polished** (glossy, saturated, darker, mirror sheen, crisp veins) → leathered (textured matte + slight sheen).
- **Game-idiom caveat:** Minecraft "polished" variants (andesite/diorite/granite/blackstone/deepslate) are
  stylised as **smooth + even-toned + geometric** (sometimes slightly *lighter*, flatter) — NOT literally
  darker/glossy — because the engine has no real specular.

## Candidate lessons (before → after)
1. **Polish = darker + more saturated base + ONE specular sheen streak; raw = lighter, desaturated, matte
   (no specular).** The darkening is a diffuse→specular shift, not pigment.
2. **Polish reveals structure — sharpen grain/vein contrast on the polished version; blur/hide it on the
   raw** (raw pitted surface obscures the pattern; polished shows it at max definition).
3. **The finish spectrum is a value + busyness + edge progression** — raw (pale, matte, busy/pitted,
   broken rounded edges) → cut (flat geometric faces, matte) → honed (smooth even, low busyness) →
   polished (darker, saturated, sharp veins, sheen, crisp edges). Author a stone's variants along this axis.
4. **Edges tell the finish** — raw = irregular/broken/rounded; cut = sharp straight geometric; polished =
   crisp + a bright bevel/rim highlight.
5. **Game-idiom caveat** — Minecraft "polished" reads as smooth + even-toned + geometric (vanilla stylises
   it, sometimes lighter), not literal darkening; match that unless deliberately going for a glossy
   "gallery" look.

## For T.N.A.
