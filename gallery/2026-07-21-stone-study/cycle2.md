# Mineral cycle 2 — gem colour chemistry & optics → saturated-crystal sprite rules

Scope: why gems are coloured (chromophores), idiochromatic vs allochromatic, and how faceting/
clarity make a gem read brilliant — translated to pixel rules for gems, aspect crystals, and ores.

## Findings
- **Colour = a trace chromophore.** Transition-metal ions (**Cr, Fe, V, Ti, Mn, Cu**) at ppm levels cause
  vivid colour by selectively absorbing wavelengths. **Cr → red (ruby) OR green (emerald)** depending on
  the host lattice (corundum vs beryl); **Fe²⁺–Ti⁴⁺ charge transfer → blue sapphire**; Cu → blue-green
  (turquoise); Mn → pink/red. The SAME element can give different hues in different hosts.
- **Idiochromatic vs allochromatic.** Idiochromatic gems (malachite, peridot, turquoise) — colour is part
  of their essential chemistry, saturated throughout. Allochromatic gems (emerald, ruby, sapphire,
  aquamarine, amethyst) — **pure form is colourless**; colour is an impurity tinting a clear body.
- **Brilliance = light return.** Sparkle = light that reflects back out through the crown to the eye.
  Facets bounce it back; "windows" let light pass through (see-through), "extinction" facets go dark.
  A real gem shows a **mix of bright, mid, and dark facets**, not a uniform tint.
- **Cut = facet strategy.** Brilliant cut (~58 facets) scatters light in all directions (max sparkle);
  **step cut** (emerald cut, few large facets) lets light pass, emphasising **colour + clarity**.
- **Clarity.** High clarity → light passes cleanly → bright/brilliant; inclusions scatter light → dull,
  cloudy, no clean extinction.

## Crystal/gem pixel lessons
1. **A gem's hue is a trace tint on a colourless base** — author most gems as a near-white/clear base +
   ONE saturated hue; the same chromophore gives different hues in different hosts, so **hue is a free
   design choice** (great for aspect colour-coding — assign each aspect a plausible gem hue).
2. **Idiochromatic vs allochromatic is a real choice** — idiochromatic (malachite/turquoise/peridot) =
   saturated colour through the whole body; allochromatic (ruby/emerald/amethyst) = colourless body
   *tinted*. Decide whether the colour fills the gem or just veils a clear core.
3. **Brilliance = a bright/dark facet MIX** — a gem reads faceted/sparkly with a few near-white "light-
   return" facets + at least one dark "extinction" facet for contrast; a uniform tint reads as flat glass,
   not a gem. (Extends the crystal shorthand: highlight facet adjacent to shadow facet.)
4. **Cut = facet count** — brilliant (many small facets, busy all-direction sparkle) vs step (few large
   flat facets, calm, shows colour). **At 16px prefer the step-cut read** (few large facets) + 1–2 specular
   sparkle points; many tiny facets read as noise.
5. **Clarity separates polished gem from raw crystal** — a clean cut gem = crisp facet edges + strong
   bright return; a raw/included crystal = softer edges, scattered internal specks, weaker highlights.
