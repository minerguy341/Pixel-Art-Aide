# Cycle 2 — Real-life trees across ecologies: crown shape & roots as biome tells

Scope: conifers (spruce/fir), savanna acacia, tropical rainforest emergents, mangrove — how
climate/light/soil drive very different shapes and spacings than temperate hardwoods (cycle 1).

## Findings
- **Conifer = conical, central-leader, packs CLOSE.** A strong apical leader (auxin suppresses
  the shoots below) makes the main stem outgrow its laterals → conical crown + defined trunk.
  Conical sheds snow and catches low-angle light; too-broad a crown would break under ice. Dense,
  tall, narrow — optimises *vertical* space, so conifers stand crowded.
- **Savanna acacia = flat-topped umbrella, stands FAR APART.** The umbrella crown captures max sun
  with tiny leaflets, filters light to lower branches (little self-shading), and shades its own root
  zone against evaporation. Water competition spaces them widely — solitary, broad, low.
- **Rainforest emergent = tall + narrow + buttress roots + crown shyness.** Trees race up for the
  canopy (tall slender boles); shallow nutrient-poor soil → **buttress roots** (plank-like flares,
  up to several m) for stability. **Crown shyness**: neighbouring canopies leave visible GAPS —
  a forest canopy is not one solid mass.
- **Mangrove = prop/stilt roots, waterlogged, dense.** Arcing stilt roots suspend the trunk above
  water (gas exchange + stability) — root *type* signals the ground itself.

## Growth→shape lessons
1. **Crown SILHOUETTE encodes the biome/light regime** — conical (snow/low-sun, dense) · rounded
   (temperate open) · flat-umbrella (arid/high-sun, spaced) · columnar-with-high-crown (crowded
   rainforest). Choose the crown outline from the climate, not by default. (Vanilla already does
   this: spruce cone, savanna acacia flat 2-tier, jungle tall high-canopy.)
2. **Apical dominance is a structural switch.** One strong central leader → *excurrent* conical
   form (conifers, saplings); leader lost → *decurrent* rounded/spreading form (mature oak, acacia).
   Draw either a single straight spire or many co-dominant forks — not a muddle of both.
3. **Root TYPE is a biome tell**: buttress flares (shallow tropical soil, giants) · prop/stilt
   (waterlogged mangrove) · deep taproot (arid) · shallow-wide (temperate). Show the root form the
   ground demands.
4. **Crown shyness → leave gaps between neighbours.** A stand of one species has channels between
   crowns; don't render a dense forest as a single seamless canopy slab.
5. **Spacing is driven by the limiting resource**: water-limited (acacia) → wide spacing, broad
   flat crowns; light-limited-but-wet (conifer/rainforest) → tight spacing, narrow tall crowns.
   Reinforces cycle 1's crown-width↔spacing link with a *why*.
